If you've been hearing that the Twin Cities housing market is crashing, the actual data might surprise you. [Inventory is up](https://www.nar.realtor/newsroom) more than 7% compared to last year, which means buyers have more choices than they've had in a while. Homes are also [taking longer to sell](https://www.johnschustergroup.com/blog/why-your-home-isnt-selling). On the surface, that can sound like the start of a downturn.

Then you look at what sellers are actually walking away with. On average, sellers are still receiving 99.3% of their original asking price. That is not what a crashing market looks like. A market in real trouble doesn't hand sellers nearly their full asking price. It hands them steep cuts and stalled listings, and that isn't what's happening here.

{% include pullquote.html %}

**Well-priced homes are still generating real activity.** In [communities like Eden Prairie, Minnetonka, and Lakeville](https://www.johnschustergroup.com/featured_communities), homes that are priced right are still drawing strong interest, especially in the move-up price ranges. Buyers have more room to be selective, so pricing and preparation matter more than they did a couple of years ago. But the demand is there for homes that are positioned well.

**What we're seeing across the Twin Cities is a balanced market, not a broken one.** Buyers have breathing room, sellers are still getting close to full price, and homes that are priced and presented correctly are still [selling](https://www.johnschustergroup.com/selling). That's a healthier market than the headlines would have you believe, and it's a very different thing from a crash.
